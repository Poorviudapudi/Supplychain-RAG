1. Purpose and scope
This handbook sets out the rules governing how Meridian Components selects, approves, monitors, and, where
necessary, exits supplier relationships. It applies to all direct material purchases, all indirect purchases above ₹1 lakh,
and all logistics and freight contracts. It does not cover the purchase of land, buildings, or insurance, which are
governed separately by the Corporate Finance Policy.
Where this handbook conflicts with a signed customer-mandated sourcing agreement, the customer agreement
prevails, and the deviation must be recorded in the deviation register maintained by the Head of Procurement.

Splitting a single requirement into multiple purchase orders in order to stay below an approval threshold is a
disciplinary offence. Annual rate contracts are approved at the value of the full contract, not the value of individual
releases against it.
4. Supplier onboarding
New suppliers must complete all seven steps below before a purchase order can be raised. The target elapsed time
from step 1 to step 7 is 21 working days.
Request for information. Capability profile, capacity declaration, customer references, and certifications
collected.
Financial health check. Minimum three years of audited financial statements. The supplier must show a debt-to
equity ratio below 2.0 and a positive net worth. A supplier failing this check may still be onboarded with the written
approval of the Chief Operating Officer and a reduced payment term.
Quality system audit. Conducted against the Meridian supplier audit checklist. A minimum score of 75 out of
100 is required. Scores between 60 and 74 permit conditional onboarding with a corrective action plan closed
within 90 days. Scores below 60 are a rejection.
Sample approval. Production part approval to Level 3, including dimensional report, material certificates, and
capability study, with a minimum process capability index of 1.33 on all designated critical characteristics.
ESG self-declaration. Covering child and forced labour, environmental permits, and conflict minerals. Mandatory
for all classes.
Contract and non-disclosure agreement. Executed on the standard Meridian template. Any deviation from the
standard terms requires Legal sign-off.
Vendor master creation. Bank details verified by Finance through an independent call-back to a number
obtained from public records, never from the supplier's own email footer.
5. Supplier performance scorecard
Suppliers are scored every quarter on a 100-point scale using the following weights:
Dimension Weight Measurement basis
On-time delivery 30% Receipts within the agreed window as a percentage of total receipts
Quality 30% Defects per million parts received, at incoming inspection and in-line
Cost 20% Adherence to agreed price and delivery of committed annual cost reduction
Responsiveness 10% Acknowledgement within 24 hours; corrective action reports within 10 working
days
Environment, social and governance 10% Annual declaration status and audit findings
Rating bands are applied to the total score:
Band Score Standing
A 90 and above Preferred — eligible for new business and long-term agreements
B 75 to 89 Approved — eligible for new business
C 60 to 74 Watch — existing business only, improvement plan required
D Below 60 At risk — business hold applies, see clause 6.4
For the on-time delivery dimension taken on its own, a supplier delivering below 90% cannot score in band A, and a
supplier delivering below 75% cannot score in band B.
6. Performance failures and consequences
6.1 On-time delivery below 90% in any quarter. A written warning is issued to the supplier within 10 working
days of quarter close, and the supplier moves to a weekly delivery review call until performance recovers above 90%
for one full quarter.
6.2 On-time delivery below 85% for two consecutive quarters. In addition to clause 6.1, a debit note equal to
2% of the quarterly invoice value is raised against the supplier, and the supplier must submit a formal
improvement plan within 15 working days. Failure to submit the plan escalates the matter to clause 6.4.
6.3 Defect rate above 500 parts per million in any quarter. The supplier bears the cost of rework at a standard
recovery rate of ₹120 per affected unit, and 100% incoming inspection is imposed at the supplier's cost until three
consecutive lots are accepted without defect.
6.4 Rating band D for two consecutive quarters. The supplier is placed on business hold. No new requests for
quotation are issued, no new part numbers are awarded, and a phase-out plan for existing parts must be prepared by
the Category Manager within 90 days.
6.5 Late delivery causing a production line stoppage. Documented downtime cost may be recovered from the
supplier, subject to a cap of 5% of the annual purchase order value for that supplier. Recovery requires a
downtime record signed by the Plant Head and a root-cause report attributing the stoppage to the supplier.
6.6 Force majeure. Clauses 6.1 to 6.5 are suspended where the supplier demonstrates a qualifying force majeure
event within 7 days of its occurrence. Port congestion, customs delays, and currency movement are expressly not
treated as force majeure.
7. Sourcing rules
7.1 Dual sourcing. Every part supplied by a supplier classified as Critical must have a qualified second source in
place within 12 months of that classification being assigned. Progress is reported monthly to the Management
Committee.
7.2 Share of business. No single supplier may hold more than 60% of the volume of any one part once dual
sourcing is established. Exceptions require the written approval of the Chief Operating Officer and are valid for one
financial year.
7.3 New country risk. Onboarding a supplier from a country in which Meridian has no existing supply base requires
Chief Operating Officer approval, supported by a written assessment of logistics, customs, and currency exposure.
7.4 Competitive bidding. All requirements above ₹25 lakh require a minimum of three comparable quotations.
Where only one supplier is technically capable, a single-source justification note must be filed and approved one level
above the normal approval authority.
8. Inventory and safety stock policy
Safety stock for every purchased part is calculated as:
Safety stock (days) = lead time in days × 0.25, subject to the minimum floors below.
Part origin and class Minimum safety stock
Imported, part supplied by a Critical supplier 30 days
Imported, all other parts 10 days
Domestic, part supplied by a Critical supplier 15 days
Domestic, all other parts 5 days
Where the calculated figure and the floor differ, the higher value applies. Safety stock levels are reviewed monthly
by the Head of Planning and may be temporarily raised with Head of Procurement approval where a named supply
risk is open.
Obsolescence provisioning is applied as follows: 50% of value for stock with no movement for 180 to 365 days, and
100% of value for stock with no movement beyond 365 days.
9. Incoterms and freight
Imports are contracted on FOB terms so that Meridian controls the freight leg and the choice of forwarder. Domestic
inbound purchases are contracted Ex-Works with freight arranged by Meridian. Exports to customers are contracted
CIF unless the customer specifies otherwise in a signed agreement.
Air freight may be used only where a written line-stoppage risk assessment exists. Planned air freight above ₹10 lakh
per month requires Head of Logistics approval; unplanned emergency air freight requires Chief Operating Officer
approval within 48 hours of despatch.
10. Escalation matrix
Level Owner Response time Typical trigger
1 Buyer 24 hours Delivery slippage of up to 3 days
2 Category Manager 48 hours Delivery slippage beyond 3 days, or a rejected lot
3 Head of Procurement 72 hours Risk of line stoppage within 7 days
4 Chief Operating Officer 5 working days Actual line stoppage, or supplier insolvency signal
Every escalation must be logged in the supplier issue register. Closing an escalation requires a documented root
cause and a verified corrective action, not merely the resumption of supply.
11. Emergency and spot purchases
An emergency purchase outside the normal process is permitted only where a production line stoppage is likely within
72 hours and no approved supplier can meet the requirement. The value is capped at ₹25 lakh per event. The
purchase must be ratified by the Head of Procurement within 5 working days, supported by the stoppage risk
assessment that justified it. More than two emergency purchases from the same supplier in one quarter triggers a
review of that supplier's classification and its safety stock setting.
12. Payment terms
Standard payment terms are 60 days from the date of goods receipt. An early payment discount of 1.5% may be
offered for payment within 15 days, at the discretion of Finance. Suppliers registered as micro or small enterprises are
paid within 45 days in accordance with statutory requirements, and this term may not be negotiated downward. Any
deviation from standard terms requires joint approval from the Head of Procurement and the Head of Finance.
